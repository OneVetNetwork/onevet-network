About OneVet Network

Veterans shouldn't need a caseworker just to find out what help exists. We put programs from the VA, state agencies and nonprofits in one searchable place.

Why we built this

Benefits for veterans are spread across dozens of websites. Federal programs live on VA.gov, state benefits sit with each state's veterans department, and some of the most useful help comes from local nonprofits that are hard to find unless someone tells you about them.

Many veterans miss out on programs they qualify for simply because they never heard of them. OneVet Network collects those programs in one directory, matches them to your service history, and tells you up front whether you're likely to qualify.

We don't replace your VA representative or a Veterans Service Officer. We help you show up to that conversation already knowing what to ask for.

Common questions

Is OneVet Network part of the VA?

No. We are an independent directory. To apply for VA benefits, you'll still go through VA.gov, a VA office, or an accredited Veterans Service Officer.

Do I need an account?

No. You can search and get matched without signing in. An account lets you save programs, keep your eligibility results, and email them to yourself.

How accurate are the eligibility results?

They're based on the rules each program publishes and the details in your profile. Treat them as a strong starting point. The program itself makes the final decision.

Can family members use the site?

Yes. Spouses, children and caregivers can search for programs, and there's a Family topic for benefits meant for them.

I found a program that's wrong or out of date. What do I do?

Use the contact form below and include the program name. We'll check it against the source and update the listing.

Contact us

Questions about the site, a listing that needs fixing, or feedback on how we can do better. We read every message.
